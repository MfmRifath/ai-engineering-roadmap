# A5 — Text Clustering and Topic Modeling

> **Thesis:** Embed, reduce, cluster, label. Four modular steps, each swappable — and that
> modularity is worth more than any particular algorithm choice.

## Why this chapter matters

Marked **Skimmable**, but it is the most direct application of
[G8](../../01-hands-on-ml-geron/notes/ch08.md) and
[G9](../../01-hands-on-ml-geron/notes/ch09.md) to text, and it teaches a genuinely useful
production skill: **making sense of a pile of unlabeled text.** Support tickets, user feedback,
search queries, error logs — knowing what is in there before you decide what to build is real
work, and this is how you do it.

Maarten Grootendorst wrote BERTopic, so this is the author's own tool explained by its author.

## Core concepts

### The pipeline

```
documents → embed → reduce dimensionality → cluster → label
             (SBERT)      (UMAP)            (HDBSCAN)   (c-TF-IDF / LLM)
```

Each stage is independently replaceable. That is the design insight — **BERTopic is a framework,
not an algorithm**, and understanding it as four swappable Lego blocks is more valuable than
memorizing the defaults.

### Step 1 — Embed

Use a sentence-embedding model ([A2](ch02.md), [A10](ch10.md)). Choose based on your domain and
your language; a general-purpose model on specialized text is the most common quality problem
here.

### Step 2 — Reduce dimensionality

Clustering in 384 or 768 dimensions suffers the curse of dimensionality
([G8](../../01-hands-on-ml-geron/notes/ch08.md)) — distances stop discriminating, and
density-based clustering in particular needs meaningful density.

**UMAP** to 5–15 dimensions. Not 2 — that is for *plotting*, and it discards far too much for
clustering. This distinction matters and is routinely got wrong.

Why UMAP over PCA here: the structure in embedding space is nonlinear, and UMAP preserves local
neighbourhoods better. Why UMAP over t-SNE: it is faster, preserves more global structure, and
can `transform` new points.

### Step 3 — Cluster

**HDBSCAN** — hierarchical DBSCAN. Its advantages over the
[G9](../../01-hands-on-ml-geron/notes/ch09.md) alternatives:

- **No k required.** You cannot know how many topics are in a support inbox.
- **Handles varying density**, unlike plain DBSCAN's single ε.
- **Marks outliers as noise** (label −1) rather than forcing every document into a cluster.

That last point is the important one and people fight it: documents that belong to no topic
*should* be labeled as such. Forcing them into clusters pollutes every topic.

### Step 4 — Label

**c-TF-IDF** is the clever bit. Ordinary TF-IDF finds words distinctive to a *document*. c-TF-IDF
concatenates all documents in a cluster into one meta-document, then finds words distinctive to
that *cluster* relative to other clusters. The top terms become the topic label.

Cheap, deterministic, and interpretable. It produces labels like `["refund", "charge",
"cancelled", "billing"]` — a keyword list, not a phrase.

**LLM-generated labels** turn that keyword list into a readable name ("Billing and refund
requests"). Alammar's "text generation Lego block" section shows this. The right division of
labor: use the cheap statistical method to *find* the topic, the expensive model to *name* it —
one LLM call per topic rather than per document.

### Representation tuning

BERTopic's other blocks refine the labels: **KeyBERT** re-ranks terms by embedding similarity to
the cluster, **MMR** (maximal marginal relevance) diversifies terms so you do not get five
synonyms, and part-of-speech filtering keeps nouns.

MMR is worth noting because it reappears in RAG: the same "relevant *and* diverse" objective is
used to avoid retrieving five near-identical chunks
([H6](../../04-ai-engineering-huyen/notes/ch06.md)).

## Key code

```python
from bertopic import BERTopic
from sentence_transformers import SentenceTransformer
from umap import UMAP
from hdbscan import HDBSCAN

# Every stage is explicit and swappable — that's the point of the design
topic_model = BERTopic(
    embedding_model=SentenceTransformer("all-MiniLM-L6-v2"),
    umap_model=UMAP(n_components=5, n_neighbors=15, metric="cosine"),  # 5, not 2
    hdbscan_model=HDBSCAN(min_cluster_size=15, metric="euclidean",
                          cluster_selection_method="eom"),
    verbose=True,
)
topics, probs = topic_model.fit_transform(docs)

topic_model.get_topic_info()      # -1 is the outlier "topic" — that's correct behaviour
topic_model.get_topic(0)          # c-TF-IDF terms for topic 0
```

## Gotchas

- **Reducing to 2 dimensions before clustering.** 2D is for plots. Cluster in 5–15.
- **Trying to eliminate the −1 outlier cluster.** Documents that fit no topic exist. Forcing them
  in degrades every cluster. (BERTopic offers `reduce_outliers` if you truly need full coverage.)
- **`min_cluster_size` left at the default.** It is the single most influential parameter and it
  depends entirely on your corpus size. Tune it first.
- **Assuming the topics are stable.** UMAP and HDBSCAN are stochastic; set `random_state` in UMAP
  or your topics change between runs. This is very confusing in a report.
- **A general embedding model on specialized text.** Medical or legal corpora need a domain
  model, or the clusters will be shallow.
- **Reading the 2D visualization as truth.** Same warning as
  [G8](../../01-hands-on-ml-geron/notes/ch08.md) — cluster sizes and distances in the plot are
  artifacts.
- **Calling an LLM per document to label topics.** Call it once per topic.

## Exercises

**Understand**
1. Why reduce dimensionality before clustering text embeddings?
2. Why is HDBSCAN a better fit than K-Means for topic discovery?
3. How does c-TF-IDF differ from ordinary TF-IDF?
4. Why should outlier documents keep the −1 label?

**Build**
1. Cluster a corpus you actually care about — your own notes, support tickets, GitHub issues,
   arXiv abstracts. Real data makes the exercise worth doing.
2. Sweep `min_cluster_size` over [5, 15, 50, 100] and look at how topic count and coherence move.
3. Compare c-TF-IDF keyword labels against LLM-generated names for the same topics. Note the cost
   difference.
4. Swap UMAP for PCA, and HDBSCAN for K-Means, one at a time. Four combinations, one comparison
   table — this demonstrates the modularity concretely.

## Flashcards

<!-- card -->
Q: What are the four stages of the BERTopic pipeline?
A: Embed (SBERT) → reduce dimensionality (UMAP) → cluster (HDBSCAN) → label (c-TF-IDF, optionally refined by an LLM). Every stage is independently swappable — it is a framework, not an algorithm.
<!-- /card -->

<!-- card -->
Q: Why reduce to 5–15 dimensions rather than 2 before clustering?
A: 2D is for visualization and discards far too much structure. Density-based clustering needs meaningful density, which 2D projection destroys — but full 384/768-dim space suffers the curse of dimensionality. 5–15 is the working range.
<!-- /card -->

<!-- card -->
Q: Why HDBSCAN rather than K-Means for topic modeling?
A: It does not require you to specify the number of topics (which you cannot know), handles clusters of varying density, and labels documents that belong to no topic as noise (−1) rather than forcing them into a cluster.
<!-- /card -->

<!-- card -->
Q: What is c-TF-IDF?
A: TF-IDF applied at cluster level: concatenate all documents in a cluster into one meta-document, then find terms distinctive to that cluster relative to other clusters. It yields cheap, deterministic, interpretable topic keywords.
<!-- /card -->

<!-- card -->
Q: What is MMR and where else does it appear?
A: Maximal marginal relevance — select items that are relevant *and* mutually diverse. Used here to avoid five synonyms in a topic label, and in RAG to avoid retrieving five near-identical chunks.
<!-- /card -->
